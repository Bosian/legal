Refunds and right of withdrawal (EU / EEA / UK)

Trader: Bo-Sian Liou (publishing as Bosian; individual trader)
Published: 7 November 2026　·　Effective: 7 November 2026

This page explains the right of withdrawal and refund arrangements for Products obtained for payment (paid downloads, and non-consumable in-app purchases) in the European Union (EU), the European Economic Area (EEA) and the United Kingdom (UK). It is an explanatory page for section 7 of the Terms of Service; in case of inconsistency, the Terms of Service prevail.

Who this applies to

  • Applies: consumers who obtained a Product for payment from a store in the EU／EEA／UK (paid downloads, and non-consumable in-app purchases).
  • Does not apply: Products obtained free of charge (including advertising-supported editions). A free download is not a sales contract, so no right of withdrawal arises. Consumable in-app purchases that have been used are handled under section 5 of the Terms of Service.

Your statutory right of withdrawal

Products obtained for payment are digital content which is not supplied on a tangible medium. Under the EU Consumer Rights Directive (Directive 2011/83/EU):

  • Article 9(1): consumers have 14 days to withdraw without giving any reason;
  • Article 9(2)(c): for digital content not supplied on a tangible medium, the withdrawal period runs from the date of the conclusion of the contract;
  • Article 16(m): the right of withdrawal is extinguished if performance has begun with the consumer's prior express consent and the consumer's acknowledgement that he thereby loses that right;
  • Article 8(7)(b) and Article 14(4)(b): the trader must provide contract confirmation on another durable medium, including that express consent and acknowledgement; if this is not done, the consumer's remedies still apply.

The corresponding UK provisions are regulations 30 (14-day right to cancel) and 37 (the digital-content exception and the conditions for losing it) of the Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013.

The refund the trader voluntarily grants

The exception above requires you to give a separate express consent and acknowledgement at the moment of purchase (for example by ticking a checkbox). However, the checkout process is provided entirely by the app store, so the trader cannot obtain that separate statement in the checkout screen; and under the European Commission's guidance, substituting "I have read and accept the terms and conditions" is not sufficient to establish the Article 16(m) exception.

The trader therefore voluntarily grants a refund within 14 days of the date of purchase. This is an arrangement the trader offers proactively. It is not an exclusion of your statutory right of withdrawal, and you are not asked to give up any other right in exchange for it.

Model wording suggested by the European Commission (for reference, to show the purpose of this arrangement):

"I hereby consent to immediate performance of the contract and acknowledge that I will lose my right of withdrawal from the contract once the download or streaming of the digital content has begun."

How to request a refund

  1. Submit a request through the refund process of the store you use (on Google Play: via your Google Play order history, or via the developer contact details listed on the store listing page); and/or
  2. Contact the trader directly using the trader contact details listed on the store listing page, stating the purchase date and order details.

How refunds are processed

  • An approved refund is issued within 14 days;
  • using the same means of payment as the original purchase;
  • with no additional charges to you.

Rights that are not limited by this page

Nothing on this page or in the Terms of Service limits or excludes:

  • your rights under applicable law where the Product is defective, does not work as reasonably expected, or does not match its description on the store listing page;
  • consumer rights which cannot be excluded or limited under applicable law; and
  • your other statutory rights as a consumer in the EU／EEA／UK.

About store refund mechanisms. The app store itself may offer its own refund window and process (for example Google Play's short refund window). Store mechanisms operate in parallel with the voluntary refund on this page; you may use either or both. The existence of a store mechanism does not reduce any right you may assert against the trader under applicable law.
